host — Dennis
=============

USE WHEN
  A claim needs a person attached to it. Dennis is the one carrying the
  argument; the data plates are what he is pointing at. A frame with a chart
  and no host is a document, which is correct for the middle of an
  explanation and wrong for its start and end.

  He is 9:16 only, on purpose. He is composited onto a room plate at the
  room's host-anchor slot in both aspects — never rescaled to fill 16:9.

MEMBERS (6 poses x 5 frames = 30)
  leaning-on-desk         the default. Talking to camera, weight on one arm.
  hands-in-pockets        listening, or between arguments. Neutral, low energy.
  holding-a-page          reading a figure out. Use when the page is the
                          source of the number on screen this beat.
  pointing-down-at-desk   the number is on the desk. Pairs with a table or
                          chart plate placed in the lower half of frame.
  head-in-hands           the cost of being right. Once per video, at most.
                          Weary, not comic — do not use it for a mild loss.
  walking-out-of-frame    the exit. Last host frame of a section, never mid-
                          argument.

  Per pose: the base plate, talk_f01 / talk_f02, idle_f01 / idle_f02.
    base       hold frame. Use on a still beat, or as the frame under a cut.
               Boil 0 — the one frame that does not tremble.
    talk_f01/2 alternate on speech, 8fps on twos. f01 has the mouth open.
               Two frames is the whole loop; do not interpolate a third.
    idle_f01/2 alternate when he is on screen and silent. Same 8fps cut; f02
               bobs 3 canvas units. Slower cadence than talk in practice —
               hold each frame two beats rather than one.

BOIL
  The loops boil: each frame is the same drawing redrawn with every stroke seed
  shifted, so tremor, overshoot and hatch phase move while geometry, line
  weight and hatch density stay put. Indices are in the manifest's loops.boil
  block — base 0, talk 1 and 2, idle 3 and 4.

  Cut the frames hard. No cross-fade, ever: a dissolve between two boil frames
  reads as a camera artefact, not as a hand redrawing a line.

  If the figure appears to shift position or change weight between frames, the
  boil has become a second drawing — that is a bug in the plate, not a
  cadence problem. The board's live boil check is there to catch exactly this.

CONTRAST — the rule that decides everything else
  Dennis is the highest-contrast object in any frame he is in. He is the reason
  people are watching, and a figure at the same value as the desk behind him
  reads as part of the furniture.

  His clothing carries real value: a mid-slate torso and near-black trousers,
  with outlines heavier than anything in the set. He was cream on cream for two
  revisions — a #D9CFBB shirt against a #E6DDC9 ground is a four percent step,
  so the largest shape in the frame had no edge at all.

  The HEAD carries the heaviest outline on the plate, and the skin is a value
  with somewhere to go rather than a tint of the ground. That is deliberate and
  it is the last thing that was wrong: with the shirt and trousers fixed but the
  head still cream on a cream wall, the eye landed on his chest instead of his
  face. A jaw shadow and a turned plane on the skull do the modelling.

  He is NOT lit to match the room. That sounds wrong and is not: an earlier
  revision tinted him per limb from the room's two sources, and the result was
  a figure sitting at exactly the desk's value. What separates him is that his
  own material hatch runs heavier than anything in the set, and each part
  carries a neutral ink pass on its turned side. The room's heaviest furniture
  is capped below him on purpose — the set gives way to the host, never the
  other way round.

  His contact pool is small and tight, so he stands ON the floor line. It is the
  one thing on the plate allowed to go darker than the line.

  The test: composite him onto a room plate and look at the two together. If
  your eye does not go to him first, the room is too loud — fix the ROOM.

WARDROBE (5 outfits)
  He hosts every episode, and a single outfit across a series reads as a
  uniform. The outfit is an episode-level argument, not a redraw: same body,
  same poses, same seeds, different clothes.

    shirt      the default. Open collar over a tee, sleeves down.
    cardigan   an olive cardigan over the shirt, carried down the upper arm.
               The been-here-since-six look.
    rolled     the same shirt, sleeves rolled to the elbow — bare forearms and
               a heavy fold band at the crease. For the working passages.
    jumper     a crew jumper, no collar. The darkest top in the set.
    gilet      a gilet over the shirt, for anything shot in the cold office.

  All five keep a mid-to-dark torso and the darkest cloth on the plate at the
  trousers, because that is what separates him from a pale wall. They vary in
  HUE and in detail, barely in value — an outfit that goes pale puts him back at
  the desk's value, which is the defect two whole revisions were spent fixing.

  EVERY FRAME OF ONE EPISODE MUST USE THE SAME OUTFIT. The 30 shipped frames are
  "shirt"; render another with BUILD.drawWith(item, "cardigan"). Mixing values
  inside an episode changes his clothes mid-sentence.

SLOTS (3, same on every one of the 30)
  figure   the whole body's bounding area. Use it to place him against the
           room's host-anchor and to know what he occludes. Nothing is
           drawn into it.

           This box's HEIGHT is what the room's host-anchor height scales to:
           scale the plate until figure.h equals the anchor's h, then sit
           floorLineY on the anchor's bottom edge. Never scale him to the
           anchor's width.
  head     head bounding area. Anchor for anything that has to sit near his
           face — a headline band, a callout, a highlight ring.
  mouth    mouth area. For lip-sync substitution if a mouth set is ever
           drawn. Empty in this batch.

SEQUENCE
  1  place figure on the room's host-anchor
  2  hold the base plate through the cut
  3  switch to talk_f01/f02 on the first word, alternate on syllable stress
  4  fall to idle_f01/f02 within two frames of the last word
  5  cut on the base plate, not mid-loop

TRAPS
  * Do not flip him horizontally to face the other way. The glasses, the part
    in the hair and the pocket line are all asymmetric; a flip reads as a
    different man. There is no lighting reason to flip him either — he is not
    lit from the room's sources, so no room angle requires a mirror.
  * head-in-hands and walking-out-of-frame do not talk. Their talk frames
    exist for continuity of the file set; using them looks like a mistake.
  * He never overlaps a plot-area. If the composition forces it, change the
    room angle, not his position.
  * Do not boil him against a boiling background. The room does not boil, on
    purpose — two trembling layers is noise, and the figure stops being the
    thing that moves.
