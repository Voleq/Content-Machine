peers — the complex
===================

USE WHEN
  The beat is "it did not move alone". A single ticker's move is an event; the
  same move across four tickers is a repricing of the whole complex, and that
  is a different claim. Use it the moment the voice-over names other companies
  — naming them and not showing them makes the audience take your word for it.

  Do NOT use it to compare a company to its peers on fundamentals. That is
  figures/compare-side (two things, one metric) or tables/numbers-sheet (one
  thing, six periods). This plate holds one moment across several names.

MEMBERS
  peer-strip-16x9   five rows. 24 slots.
  peer-strip-9x16   four rows. 20 slots. Portrait cannot hold five rows at a
                    legible move size — cut rows, never columns.

SLOTS
  unit           the moment, once ("the memory complex · today"). Never repeat
                 "today" in the rows.
  head-move      heading over the move column.
  head-fwd       heading over the multiple column.
  ticker-N       row N's ticker. Row 1 is THE SUBJECT — its role is
                 tickerSubject (structure); rows 2+ are otherParty. So the row
                 you are in is legible with no highlight, which is what keeps
                 band-N free for the row the voice-over is actually on.
  move-N         today's move. The largest figure on the plate: it is the beat.
                 Authored in `down`, because a strip is called for when a
                 complex moves together and that move is almost always red. A
                 green row is a role override on the shot:
                   boxes["move-2"] = { …rect, role: "moveUp" }
  fwd-N          forward multiple, in otherParty — it is the market's opinion,
                 not a fact about the company. Second-largest figure.
  bars           REGION, one column wide, spanning every row. The move as a
                 shape as well as a figure:
                   SERIES.rowBars({ box: slots.bars, values, rows })
                 One scale shared across the rows — that shared scale is the
                 point, and the renderer places the zero rule from the domain,
                 so when every move is red zero lands on the right-hand edge and
                 every bar runs left from it. Pass null for a row you have no
                 figure for; it draws nothing and the row keeps its rail.
  caption        one line at the foot. What the strip means, not what it says.
  band-N         row highlight. Composite overlays/row-band here.

SEQUENCE
  1  unit
  2  head-move, head-fwd
  3  tickers, top to bottom — the subject first
  4  bars, top to bottom, from the zero rule outward
  5  moves, top to bottom
  6  multiples, top to bottom
  7  band-N on the row the voice-over is on, one at a time

TRAPS
  * Two figure columns, never three. A strip carrying only the move is a fact
    with no consequence; a strip carrying three figures is a table, and a
    table is a different plate with a different job.
  * A row with no figure stays EMPTY. Do not write "—" or "n/a", and do not
    invent a move because the row looks unfinished — put the slot in the
    shot's needs[] and let the audit carry it. An empty cell means NO DATA.
  * No ground2 panel behind the rows. row-band is ground2, and a ground2 band
    on a ground2 panel is the invisible-highlight defect this library has
    already shipped once. The strip stands on rules.
  * The subject is row 1. Not row 3 because that is where it sorts by size.
  * Never order rows by the size of the move. It reads as a ranking, which is
    a claim the plate has not earned. Subject first, then the complex.
