annotations — marks made on another plate
========================================

USE WHEN
  The voice-over points at something. The script asks for these by name
  ([DOODLE: annotations/scrawl-oval-wide], [SCRIBBLE: circle -> Gross margin]);
  they are not decoration.

  An annotation is drawn in ATTENTION (#E0A016). It therefore SPENDS the frame's
  one attention. A plate that already has an attention mark cannot also be
  annotated — pick one, or split the beat into two shots. This is the whole
  reason annotations are a family and not a flag on every plate: the operator has
  to choose.

MEMBERS (10, all alpha cut-outs, stretched onto the thing they wrap)
  scrawl-oval-wide    the workhorse. Circles a line of type or a headline.
  scrawl-oval-tight   one cell, one candle, one word.
  underline-swipe     two swipes under a line. Emphasis without enclosing.
  underline-tight     the same, for a cell or a single figure.
  strike-out          two crossing swipes. For a number that is wrong or stale.
  box-scrawl          a rough rectangle. For a block, not a line.
  bracket-rows        a tall bracket plus a stub, for grouping rows. Has a note
                      slot to the right, outside the plate.
  arrow-elbow         from a scrawled note to a target. note slot at the tail.
  caret-note          a caret with words above it, for an insertion.
  tick-marks          three ticks. For a list being confirmed.

SLOTS
  area   what the mark wraps or points at. Nothing is drawn in it — it is how
         the compositor knows where to put the plate.
  note   the mark's own words, on EVERY mark. Set in the family's one type
         role, "caption": Courier Prime, in attention, sized off the pen
         (inkWeight x 2.6) so the words scale with the mark they belong to.
         Three marks used to declare this slot with a role no plate defined,
         which meant the renderer had no size or colour to set them in and the
         audit skipped them without a word.

  BOTH SLOTS ARE MEASURED OFF THE DRAWN INK, never off the geometry the strokes
  were built from. The two are not the same line: stroke() resamples, wobbles
  and overshoots what it is given, and the difference between the two is exactly
  the margin that decides whether a mark lands on the type it is marking. So:
  enclosing marks (the ovals, box-scrawl) inscribe the largest box of the
  wrapped thing's proportions that clears every point of ink; the underlines
  take their floor from the topmost ink; bracket-rows ends where the arms begin;
  the two points-at marks start a short gap past the tip. strike-out and
  tick-marks claim the whole canvas, because they are the marks that are
  supposed to cross what they mark.

  The mark goes on the TYPE, not on the slot rectangle. A table cell is 216
  canvas units tall for 30-unit figures; an oval stretched onto the rectangle is
  an oval around empty space. The compositor derives the box from the type
  metrics — if you place one by hand, do the same.

HOW A MARK LANDS
  Each mark has an "area" slot: what it wraps. The compositor solves the
  transform so area lands on the target, and the ink then falls where it was
  drawn to fall — which is how underline-swipe works without special-casing,
  its swipe being drawn below its area rather than inside it.

  Two solve modes, in the manifest:
    both       x and y independently. Only for marks that ENCLOSE — an oval is
               meant to take its target's proportions.
    x-uniform  fit the width, same scale for y. For marks whose ink is a line of
               its own natural thickness. Solving an underline's y independently
               stretches the swipe into a fat wave.

  And an anchor, for the x-uniform ones:
    bottom     underlines. Their ink is below the area, so centring would put
               the swipe through the glyph bottoms.
    middle     strikes and ticks, which cross what they mark.

  Two size classes, because line weight is a canvas-unit quantity. Wide marks
  wrap a headline or a row; tight marks wrap one cell or one word and carry
  ABSOLUTE stroke weights — fractional weights on a small canvas come out as
  hairlines at use size. Every mark declares inkWeight, and inkWeight x solve
  has to land between roughly 3.2 and 26 units. The compositor warns when it
  does not; that warning means "wrong size class", not "adjust grow".

THE SWEEP (the two ovals)
  A circled word is a SPIRAL, not a closed ellipse: 1.22 turns, opening steadily
  outward, so the finish passes OUTSIDE the start and the two cross the way a pen
  carrying on round actually crosses. That crossing is the whole read. Earlier
  versions held a constant radius and kicked the last 8% outward to fake a
  pen-lift, which put a radial dogleg in open space beside the start — a mistake,
  not a gesture. There is no second reinforcing arc either: over a 2.5-radian
  sweep it hugged the primary closely enough to read as a misregistered second
  printing. The spiral's own overlap IS the reinforcement, and it lands where a
  real one does.

  The waver is two or three slow lobes across the whole sweep; per-point noise
  reads as a lumpy potato. Both lobes are biased OUTWARD — a ring that dips
  inside its nominal radius reads no differently, and every dip is paid for twice
  in the box the mark can wrap. The ellipse is tilted a few degrees, because
  nobody draws one axis-aligned.

  Overshoot ("over") extends a stroke TANGENTIALLY past its last point, so it is
  measured at the ends where it actually falls, not charged to every angle as
  clearance.

  Geometric limit, worth knowing before you call for one: an oval wraps about
  0.55 of its own canvas width, because it has to draw outside what it encloses.
  A mark solved onto a 900-unit headline therefore needs ~1600 units of canvas —
  fine at 16:9, impossible in a 1080-wide portrait frame. Underline that headline
  instead; the compositor's "draws NNNu off the left" error is telling you this.

SEQUENCE
  Draw it on. 3-5 frames along the stroke's own direction, then hold.
  Never fade one in and never dissolve between two: a mark that appears by
  opacity reads as a camera artefact, not as a hand.
  The mark lands on its anchor word in the voice-over, not before it.

TRAPS
  * One per frame. Two marks is two attentions, which is no attention.
  * Do not annotate a row band. The band says "here" and the mark says "this" —
    together they say it twice. Band the row, then mark the cell.
  * Do not scrawl on the host. He is a character, not a chart.
  * A scrawl is not a correction. If the number is wrong, fix the number.
