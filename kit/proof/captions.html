§5 — the captions

They are on screen for one hundred percent of every video and they are the only element that does not look drawn. Everything in the frame is hand-made and then the words arrive in standard subtitle styling. It is the largest surface in the product and the least considered.

This is the one item in the brief you asked to see two options for before choosing, so that is what this page is. It is not a plate and it does not ship in the kit: captions live on the renderer's caption path, which is why this is a conversation rather than a delivery.

The two options

A · drawn container, clean typeB · hand-lettered face
what is drawn A hand-drawn band or underlay in the kit's hand — a torn paper strip with the kit's own tooth and wobble. The words stay a clean grotesque. The words themselves are the drawn element: a lettering face with per-glyph variation, no container at all.
forty minutes of it Fine. The band is furniture; the eye stops seeing it in ten seconds, which is what you want from something present the whole time. The risk. Irregular letterforms cost a little legibility per word, and that cost compounds across forty minutes in a way a still cannot show.
9:16 legibility floor Survives. Type is a clean face at whatever size the floor demands, and the band scales with it. Unproven. Hand-lettering is least legible exactly where it is smallest.
numbers Numerals stay in the kit's Courier, which is already what every figure on every plate uses — the caption agrees with the plates for free. Hand-lettered numerals are the worst case: 3/8, 5/6, 1/7 are the pairs a viewer must not confuse on a channel about figures.
build cost One drawn asset per aspect, boiled like anything else. It is a plate in everything but name. A full face, or a renderer that perturbs glyph outlines per frame. Much larger, and it is the piece most likely to still be unfinished at video forty.

My read, and it is a recommendation rather than a decision

Option A. The instinct in your brief is right: the drawn thing should be the container, not the words. The reason is not taste — it is that the caption is the only element in the frame whose job is to be read rather than looked at, and every other element earns its hand by being looked at. A hand-lettered caption spends legibility, which is the one currency this element cannot spend, in exchange for a texture nobody will consciously notice after ten seconds.

The band gets you the whole win \u2014 the words stop arriving from another piece of software \u2014 at a fraction of the cost and none of the risk. And it composes: a torn strip in the kit's hand sitting under clean Courier numerals is exactly what the plates already do.

What would change my mind: if you want the captions to be funny rather than transparent. Hand-lettering is a joke you can keep telling for forty minutes, and that is a editorial call about the channel's voice that I should not make for you.

Unverified, and it is the thing to test before committing either way: neither option here has been watched in motion for more than a few seconds, and the entire question is about what forty minutes feels like. A one-minute cut with real captions over real plates would settle it faster than any amount of argument on this page.
