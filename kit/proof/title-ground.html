§1 — the title's own ground · decided: card

DECIDED: card. engine/build.js TITLE_GROUND = "card", on all 22 chapter openers. This page is now the record of what that was chosen over: three treatments and the un-grounded control, on the two worst walls in the family and on a clean one, with the measured contrast floor inside the published title box beside each. Every number below is unchanged — it is the evidence the decision rests on.

The problem it fixes: chapter-opener titles were legible because the wall behind them happened to be flat and pale. That is a property of the wall, borrowed — not a property of the title.

The rule nothing stated. mass() suppresses every cast shadow whose base sits above 36% of frame height, with the comment "so the title box stays on flat wall and the chapter openers keep a clean ground for type". That is the only place the constraint exists, it is a side condition on a shadow pass, and it stops nothing else: a prop, a tone, a toned wall or a dusk variant all land behind the box without passing it.

And it was already broken — in the shipped library, with no new room art at all. The family audit at the foot of this page measures every plate that declares a title slot, in the pre-card state: 16 of the 22 chapter openers had drawn ink under the title box. room/whiteboard-wall-9x16 is the worst in the family at 35.02% of the box under 4.5:1, and room/whiteboard-wall-16x9 the worst in 16:9 at 28.27% — on both, the title box is over the desk, a monitor and a stack, 655 and 714 marks deep. room/printer-corner-16x9 runs the night window's fifteen blind slats behind the title. Six plates are genuinely clean, and five of those six are portrait. So this is not a risk that a dusk variant would introduce; it is a defect the borrowed rule was already failing to prevent, which is the argument for answering §1 before §4 rather than after.
